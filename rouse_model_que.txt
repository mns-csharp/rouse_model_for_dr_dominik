        ROUSE MODEL: PROPERTIES AND MATHEMATICAL EQUATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sources:
  Rouse, P.E. Jr., J. Chem. Phys. 21(7), 1272-1280 (1953)
  Kuriata, A., Gront, D. & Sikorski, A., CMST 22(4), 179-185 (2016)


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1  STATIC PROPERTIES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1  Gaussian Submolecule Probability
──────────────────────────────────────
The probability that the far end of a submolecule is found at position (x,y,z)
relative to its near end, assuming the submolecule is long enough for Gaussian
statistics to apply.

  ψ(x,y,z) dx dy dz = (β/π)^(3/2) exp[−β(x² + y² + z²)] dx dy dz

  β = 3 / (2σ²),  where σ² = mean-square submolecule end-to-end separation


1.2  Mean-Squared End-to-End Distance
──────────────────────────────────────
The average squared distance between the first and last beads of the chain,
measuring overall chain extension.

  ⟨R²⟩ = ⟨(r_N − r_1)²⟩

  Scaling:  ⟨R²⟩ ~ N^(2ν),   ν ≈ 0.588 (3D SAW with excluded volume)
  Ideal Rouse (no excluded volume):  ⟨R²⟩ = Nσ²,  i.e. ν = 0.5


1.3  Mean-Squared Radius of Gyration
─────────────────────────────────────
The average squared distance of all beads from the chain's center of mass,
a less noisy measure of chain size than R².

  ⟨R_g²⟩ = ⟨ Σ_{i=1}^{N} (r_i − r_CM)² ⟩

  Scaling:  ⟨R_g²⟩ ~ N^(2ν),  same exponent as ⟨R²⟩


1.4  Universal Ratio R² / R_g²
───────────────────────────────
A shape diagnostic ratio that is constant for a given universality class;
deviations signal sampling or equilibration problems.

  ⟨R²⟩ / ⟨R_g²⟩ ≈ 6.25   (3D self-avoiding walk)
  Ideal chain (Gaussian):  ⟨R²⟩ / ⟨R_g²⟩ = 6


1.5  Configuration Probability (Full Chain)
───────────────────────────────────────────
The joint probability density for the entire chain configuration in
3N-dimensional space, assuming independent Gaussian submolecules.

  Ψ = (β/π)^(3N/2) exp[−β Σ_{j=1}^{N} (x_j² + y_j² + z_j²)]


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
2  DYNAMIC PROPERTIES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  Rouse Matrix Eigenvalues
─────────────────────────────
Eigenvalues of the tridiagonal connectivity matrix A that couples
nearest-neighbor submolecules along the chain.

  λ_p = 4 sin²[ pπ / (2(N+1)) ],    p = 1, 2, …, N


2.2  Relaxation Times (Exact)
─────────────────────────────
Each normal mode p has a characteristic relaxation time governing how quickly
that mode of coordinated segmental motion decays.

  τ_p = σ² / [ 24 B k T  sin²( pπ / (2(N+1)) ) ],    p = 1, 2, …, N

  B = mobility of the end of a submolecule
  k = Boltzmann constant;  T = temperature


2.3  Relaxation Times (Long-Wavelength Approximation, p ≪ N)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
For low mode numbers the sine can be approximated, yielding relaxation times
independent of how the chain is subdivided.

  τ_p ≈ σ² N² / (6π² p² B k T)

  Equivalently using measurable quantities:
  τ_p = 6(η₀ − η_s) / (π² p² n k T)

  where η₀ = steady-flow solution viscosity, η_s = solvent viscosity,
        n = number density of polymer molecules


2.4  Longest Relaxation Time Scaling
────────────────────────────────────
The slowest mode (p = 1) sets the overall chain relaxation timescale; its
scaling with N determines how simulation cost grows with chain length.

  τ_R = τ_1 ~ N^(1+2ν)

  Ideal Rouse (ν = 0.5):  τ_R ~ N²
  With excluded volume (ν ≈ 0.588):  τ_R ~ N^2.18


2.5  Steady-Flow Viscosity
──────────────────────────
The zero-frequency viscosity of the polymer solution, combining the solvent
contribution with the summed contributions of all relaxation modes.

  η₀ = η_s + nσ² N(N+2) / (36 B)


2.6  Complex Viscosity Components
─────────────────────────────────
The real part η₁ gives the in-phase (dissipative) response and η₂ gives the
out-of-phase (elastic) response under oscillatory shear.

  η₁ = η_s + n k T  Σ_{p=1}^{N}  τ_p / (1 + ω² τ_p²)

  η₂ = n k T  Σ_{p=1}^{N}  ω τ_p² / (1 + ω² τ_p²)

  η* = η₁ − iη₂  (complex viscosity);  ω = angular frequency


2.7  Complex Shear Modulus Components
─────────────────────────────────────
The storage modulus G₁ quantifies elastic energy stored per cycle and the
loss modulus G₂ quantifies energy dissipated per cycle.

  G* = G₁ + i G₂ = iω η*

  G₁ = n k T  Σ_{p=1}^{N}  ω² τ_p² / (1 + ω² τ_p²)

  G₂ = ω η_s + n k T  Σ_{p=1}^{N}  ω τ_p / (1 + ω² τ_p²)


2.8  High-Frequency Approximations
───────────────────────────────────
Closed-form expressions valid when many modes contribute, obtained by
replacing the discrete mode sum with an integral.

  η₁ ≈ η_s + 3(η₀ − η_s) / [π (2ωτ₁)^(1/2)],    2 < ωτ₁ < N²/250

  G₁ ≈ n k T [ (π/2)(ωτ₁/2)^(1/2) − 0.5 ],       5 < ωτ₁ < N²/250


2.9  Diffusion Coefficient
──────────────────────────
The rate at which the chain's center of mass diffuses through the medium,
inversely proportional to chain length because total friction scales
linearly with N.

  D = k_B T / (N ζ)

  Equivalently:  D = ⟨(r(t) − r(0))²⟩ / (6t)

  Scaling:  D ~ N^(−1)   (Rouse prediction)


2.10  Center-of-Mass Mean Square Displacement
─────────────────────────────────────────────
The center of mass undergoes normal (Fickian) diffusion at all times,
growing linearly with time.

  g_CM(t) = ⟨(r_CM(t) − r_CM(0))²⟩ = 6 D t  ~  t^1


2.11  Middle-Segment Mean Square Displacement
─────────────────────────────────────────────
A single interior monomer shows sub-diffusive motion at short times due to
chain connectivity constraints, crossing over to free diffusion once the
chain has fully relaxed.

  g₁(t) = ⟨(r_{N/2}(t) − r_{N/2}(0))²⟩

  g₁(t) ~ t^(1/2)     for t < τ_R   (sub-diffusive, internal modes)
  g₁(t) ~ t^1         for t ≥ τ_R   (free diffusion)


2.12  End-to-End Vector Autocorrelation
───────────────────────────────────────
Measures how quickly the chain loses memory of its initial end-to-end
orientation; the decay time defines the longest relaxation time τ_R.

  g_R(t) = ⟨R(0) · R(t)⟩ / ⟨R²⟩

  g_R(t) ~ exp(−t / τ_R)

  τ_R extracted at g_R(τ_R) = 1/e


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3  MODEL POTENTIALS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1  Harmonic Bond (Bead-Spring / Rouse Model)
──────────────────────────────────────────────
Nearest-neighbor beads are connected by harmonic springs that penalize
deviations from an equilibrium bond length.

  U_ij^S = k (l_ij − l₀)²

  l₀ = equilibrium bond length;  k = spring constant


3.2  Excluded Volume (Athermal Hard-Core)
─────────────────────────────────────────
A purely repulsive step-function potential that prevents bead overlap,
making the system athermal.

  U_ij = E   for r_ij ≤ d_k
  U_ij = 0   for r_ij > d_k

  d_k = bead diameter;  E = repulsive energy barrier


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
4  SUMMARY OF KEY SCALING LAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Property              Scaling Law      Rouse Exponent
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ⟨R²⟩, ⟨R_g²⟩         ~ N^(2ν)        2ν = 1.0 (ideal); ≈1.18 (SAW)
  R² / R_g² ratio       ≈ const         6 (ideal); ≈6.25 (SAW)
  τ_R (longest relax.)  ~ N^(1+2ν)      2.0 (ideal); ≈2.18 (SAW)
  D (diffusion coeff.)  ~ N^(−1)        −1
  g_CM(t)               ~ t^1           1
  g₁(t), t < τ_R        ~ t^(1/2)       0.5

   PROPERTIES VALIDATED BY KURIATA, GRONT & SIKORSKI, CMST 22(4) 179-185 (2016)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The authors used two off-lattice coarse-grained models (bead-spring Rouse model
and fixed-bond-length backrub model) with athermal excluded volume to validate
the following Rouse-model properties via Monte Carlo simulation.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1  STATIC PROPERTIES VALIDATED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1  Mean-Squared End-to-End Distance Scaling
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Both models reproduce the expected power-law growth of chain size with chain
length, confirming self-avoiding walk universality.

  ⟨R²⟩ ~ N^(2ν)

  Measured exponent:  2ν = 1.20  (both models)
  Theory (3D SAW):   2ν ≈ 1.18


1.2  Mean-Squared Radius of Gyration Scaling
────────────────────────────────────────────
The radius of gyration scales with the same exponent as the end-to-end
distance, providing an independent confirmation of correct static scaling.

  ⟨R_g²⟩ ~ N^(2ν)

  Measured exponent:  2ν = 1.20  (both models)
  Theory (3D SAW):   2ν ≈ 1.18


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
2  DYNAMIC PROPERTIES VALIDATED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  Center-of-Mass Mean Square Displacement
────────────────────────────────────────────
The center of mass exhibits normal Fickian diffusion (linear in time) for
both algorithms, confirming that the pseudo-time scale is properly defined.

  g_CM(t) = ⟨(r_CM(t) − r_CM(0))²⟩ ~ t^1

  Measured exponent:  1.00  (backrub);  0.95  (Rouse bead-spring)
  Theory:             1.00


2.2  Middle-Segment Mean Square Displacement
────────────────────────────────────────────
The middle monomer shows two distinct time regimes: sub-diffusive at short
times due to chain connectivity, crossing over to free diffusion after the
longest relaxation time.

  g₁(t) = ⟨(r_{N/2}(t) − r_{N/2}(0))²⟩

  g₁(t) ~ t^(1/2)   for t < τ_R
  g₁(t) ~ t^1        for t ≥ τ_R

  Measured short-time exponent:  0.57  (backrub);  0.60  (Rouse bead-spring)
  Theory:                        0.50
  Note: Slightly higher than 0.5 due to excluded volume effects.


2.3  Diffusion Coefficient Scaling
──────────────────────────────────
The diffusion coefficient decreases inversely with chain length, as predicted
by the Rouse model where total friction is proportional to N.

  D = ⟨(r(t) − r(0))²⟩ / (6t)

  D ~ N^(−1)

  Measured exponent:  −1.00  (backrub);  −1.01  (Rouse bead-spring)
  Theory:             −1.00


2.4  Longest Relaxation Time Scaling
────────────────────────────────────
The end-to-end vector autocorrelation decay time scales as a power law with
chain length, with the exponent close to the Rouse prediction for excluded-
volume chains.

  g_R(t) = ⟨R(0) · R(t)⟩ / ⟨R²⟩  ~  exp(−t / τ_R)

  τ_R ~ N^(1+2ν)

  Measured exponent:  2.13  (backrub);  2.30  (Rouse bead-spring)
  Theory (SAW):      ≈2.18

3  SUMMARY TABLE — MEASURED vs THEORETICAL EXPONENTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
